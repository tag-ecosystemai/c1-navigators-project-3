# Official Public Regulatory Policy Document: US OCC Consumer Credit Examination Standards
## Document Reference: Comptroller's Handbook — Consumer Lending (Credit Risk Management)
**Issuing Agency:** Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC), US Department of the Treasury  
**Regulatory Framework:** Federal Reserve Act, 12 CFR Part 30 (Safety and Soundness Standards)  
**Applicability:** National Banks, Federal Savings Associations, Consumer Credit Examiners  
**Target RAG Layer:** Generative AI Agent Credit Risk Underwriting & Policy Exception Management  

---

### 1. Fundamental Credit Risk Principles
National bank supervisory guidelines require that credit granting activities follow prudent, well-defined underwriting policies. The core objective of loan investigation is to establish the borrower's **primary source of repayment** (operating cash flow / recurring wages) rather than relying primarily on secondary sources (collateral liquidation or co-signers).

---

### 2. Core Underwriting Standards & Evaluation Factors

#### A. Capacity & Cash-Flow Analysis
* **Primary Source of Repayment:** For consumer installment loans, the primary source of repayment is the borrower's verifiable disposable cash flow.
* **Debt Burden (DTI) Standards:**
  * Policies must establish reasonable upper limits on borrower debt-to-income (DTI) ratios.
  * Standard benchmark: A total DTI exceeding **40% to 45%** represents an elevated credit risk and must require formal documented compensating factors.
  * DTI exceeding **50%** is viewed as excessive debt burden under supervisory standards.
* **Residual Income Buffer:** Examiners assess whether the borrower retains an adequate dollar cushion (residual income) after all living and debt obligations to withstand unforeseen shocks.

#### B. Credit History & Credit Bureau Tiering
* **Credit Score Validation:** Banks utilize credit bureau scoring models (FICO / VantageScore) as an objective predictor of default probability.
* **Tier Stratification:**
  * **Prime (Scores 720+):** Low default probability; streamlined verification permissible.
  * **Near-Prime (Scores 660–719):** Acceptable credit; standard verification required.
  * **Subprime (Scores < 660):** Elevated default hazard; requires comprehensive cash flow and bank statement verification.
  * **Adverse Records:** Active bankruptcies, unsatisfied civil judgments, or severe past delinquencies (90+ days past due) represent disqualifying safety-and-soundness risks.

#### C. Verification & Fraud Prevention
* **Verification of Financial Information:** Policies must require verification of income, employment, and liabilities through independent, documented sources.
* **Bank Statement Audit:** Review of bank account statements is essential to verify:
  * Regularity and origin of salary deposits.
  * Undisclosed liabilities or hidden debt service outlays.
  * Abnormal or unexplained lump-sum deposits that artificially inflate account balances immediately prior to application ("window dressing").

---

### 3. Policy Exception & Referral Management
* Every bank must maintain a formal exception-tracking process for applications that breach established underwriting thresholds (e.g., elevated DTI, low credit score, or insufficient employment tenure).
* Applications with policy exceptions must be formally referred to a credit supervisor or underwriting committee for detailed investigation and senior sign-off.
