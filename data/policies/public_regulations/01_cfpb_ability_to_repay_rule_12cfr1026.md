# Official Public Regulatory Policy Document: US CFPB Ability-to-Repay & Underwriting Rules
## Regulatory Code: 12 CFR § 1026.43 (Regulation Z)
**Issuing Agency:** Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB)  
**Statutory Authority:** Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act (P.L. 111-203), Truth in Lending Act (15 U.S.C. 1639c)  
**Applicability:** Consumer Credit Underwriting, Ability-to-Repay Verification, Debt-to-Income Standards  
**Target RAG Layer:** Generative AI Agent Policy Verification & Compliance Auditing

### 1. General Ability-to-Repay (ATR) Rule Overview (§ 1026.43(c)(1))
Under federal statutory standards, a financial institution shall not make a consumer loan or credit extension unless the creditor makes a reasonable and good faith determination at or before consummation that the consumer will have a reasonable ability to repay the loan according to its terms.

### 2. Mandatory Underwriting Factors (§ 1026.43(c)(2))
In determining whether an applicant satisfies the Ability-to-Repay requirement, the credit underwriting system and analyst must verify and consider eight mandatory statutory factors:

1. **Current or Reasonably Expected Income or Assets (§ 1026.43(c)(2)(i)):**
   * The creditor must evaluate current or reasonably expected income or assets (other than the value of the dwelling or unpledged collateral).
   * Income cannot be merely stated; it must be verified using reasonably reliable third-party records (e.g., W-2s, tax returns, bank transaction statements showing payroll credits).

2. **Current Employment Status (§ 1026.43(c)(2)(ii)):**
   * If the creditor relies on employment income in assessing repayment capacity, the creditor must verify the consumer's current employment status.
   * Employment stability, length of tenure, and certainty of continuation must be confirmed.

3. **Monthly Payment on the Covered Credit Transaction (§ 1026.43(c)(2)(iii)):**
   * The creditor must calculate the scheduled monthly repayment installment using the fully indexed interest rate and substantially equal monthly payments that amortize the loan principal over the loan term.

4. **Monthly Payment on Simultaneous Loans (§ 1026.43(c)(2)(iv)):**
   * The creditor must determine the monthly payment on any simultaneous credit transactions or subordinate financing that the creditor knows or has reason to know will be incurred by the consumer.

5. **Monthly Payment for Ongoing Housing Expenses (§ 1026.43(c)(2)(v)):**
   * The creditor must verify monthly housing obligations, including rent payments, mortgage principal/interest, property taxes, and mandatory insurance.

6. **Current Debt Obligations, Alimony, and Child Support (§ 1026.43(c)(2)(vi)):**
   * The creditor must identify and calculate all current debt obligations, revolving lines, installment loans, and statutory maintenance commitments.
   * Undisclosed debt service obligations identified via banking transactions must be factored into debt servicing calculations.

7. **Monthly Debt-to-Income (DTI) Ratio or Residual Income (§ 1026.43(c)(2)(vii)):**
   * The creditor must calculate the ratio of total monthly debt commitments to verified gross or net monthly income.
   * Under regulatory safe harbor standards, total DTI should not exceed **43.0%** unless documented compensating factors (e.g., substantial verified liquid financial reserves, pristine bureau history) are formally recorded.

8. **Credit History (§ 1026.43(c)(2)(viii)):**
   * The creditor must review the consumer's credit history through a credit bureau report or alternative credit data, reviewing adverse public records, delinquencies, and bankruptcies.

### 3. Verification Standards Using Third-Party Records (§ 1026.43(c)(3))
* **Prohibition on Stated-Income Lending:** A creditor may not rely on unverified verbal or written statements from the consumer regarding income or assets.
* **Transaction Record Verification:** Consecutive 60-day to 90-day bank deposit records are recognized as valid third-party records for income verification. Inconsistencies between stated application figures and actual bank deposits exceeding 10% constitute an underwriting discrepancy requiring secondary documentation.
