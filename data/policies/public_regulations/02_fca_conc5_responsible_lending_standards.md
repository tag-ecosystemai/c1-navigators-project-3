# Official Public Regulatory Policy Document: UK FCA Responsible Lending & Affordability Standards
## Regulatory Code: FCA Handbook CONC Chapter 5.2A (Creditworthiness Assessment)
**Issuing Authority:** UK Financial Conduct Authority (FCA)  
**Legal Framework:** Financial Services and Markets Act 2000, Consumer Credit Sourcebook (CONC)  
**Applicability:** Consumer Credit Underwriting, Affordability Assessments, Vulnerability Detection  
**Target RAG Layer:** Generative AI Agent Behavioral & Transaction Risk Verification  

---

### 1. Scope and Core Obligation (CONC 5.2A.4R)
Before entering into a regulated credit agreement or significantly increasing a credit limit, a firm must undertake a reasonable assessment of the creditworthiness of the customer.

The assessment of creditworthiness must be based on sufficient information, obtained from:
1. The customer (where appropriate); and
2. A credit reference agency (where necessary); and
3. Financial records and transaction data accessible to the firm (e.g., current account history, open banking records).

---

### 2. Dual Assessment Objective: Credit Risk vs. Affordability Risk (CONC 5.2A.10R)
The creditworthiness assessment must explicitly evaluate two distinct dimensions of risk:
1. **Credit Risk (Risk to the Lender):** The risk that the customer will fail to make payments in accordance with the agreement; and
2. **Affordability Risk (Risk to the Customer):** The risk that the customer will be unable to make repayments without suffering significant adverse consequences to their basic living standards or incurring unsustainable debt.

---

### 3. Indicators of Financial Distress & Behavioral Red Flags (CONC 5.2A.15G)
In reviewing transaction histories and banking statements (covering at least the preceding 90 days), the underwriting agent must actively investigate and flag indicators of financial vulnerability and distress:

#### A. Inability to Meet Basic Commitments
* Consecutive occurrences of unpaid direct debits, returned items, or Non-Sufficient Funds (NSF) charges.
* Reliance on unauthorized overdraft facilities to meet non-discretionary living expenses (utilities, food, rent).
* Account balances persistently trending to zero or negative within several days of receiving regular income ("payday exhaustion").

#### B. High-Cost Short-Term Credit & Debt Stacking
* Recurring debits payable to high-cost short-term lenders, payday loan entities, or unmonitored Buy Now Pay Later (BNPL) schemes.
* Repeated refinancing or borrowing to service preexisting debt obligations.

#### C. Excessive Discretionary Expenditure Indicating Impaired Judgment
* High-frequency or high-volume gambling transactions (e.g., spending in excess of 10% of monthly net income on bookmakers, online casinos, or lotteries).
* Sudden, unexplained asset transfers immediately preceding a loan application.

---

### 4. Proportionate Verification & Inquiries (CONC 5.2A.20R)
* **Rule of Proportionality:** The depth of inquiry must increase where the credit amount is significant, where the customer's credit history is borderline, or where anomalies exist in bank records.
* **Income Corroboration:** Firms must independently corroborate stated income where there is reason to doubt its reliability (e.g., mismatch between declared income and regular banking credits).
